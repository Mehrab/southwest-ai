2Why decks

A document is read alone and kept. A deck is shown in a room while someone talks, so something happens in that room: a yes, a plan, a new skill.

DocumentDeck
ReaderReads alone, at their own paceListens to you, glances at the screen
WordsAll on the pageFew on the slide, the rest is what you say
GoalA record to come back toSomething happens in the room

Five jobs a deck does

ASKGet a yes: proposal, budget request, pitch to a bank
TEACHBuild a skill: training, how-to steps, orientation
REPORTShow where things stand: monthly numbers, status update
PLANGet everyone on one plan: kickoff, goals, holiday plan
RALLYMove a crowd: town hall, fundraiser, welcome

What makes a good deck

  1. One message per slide, and the title says it.
  2. The slide is for the room. The notes are for you.
  3. It ends with the ask: what you want the room to decide or do.
  4. Fewer true slides beat more impressive ones.

5Parts of a deck

Look at the deck you just built. If you can name the part, you can ask AI to change it. Which part is still empty?

  • Theme: colors, fonts and background for the whole deck.
  • Layouts: where things sit on a slide (title only, two columns, big picture).
  • Slides: the pages, in order. One message each.
  • Elements: what sits on a slide: title, text, chart, picture, icon, slide number.
  • Speaker notes: what you'll say, under each slide. Only you see them.

Slide types

Title · Agenda · Section divider · Big number · Chart · Comparison · Picture · Closing ask. Name the type in your prompt: “Make slide 3 a comparison slide.”

6Modify your deck in three steps

Stay in the same Claude chat. One request per step. Each step gives you a new version of the file.

Stuck on how to ask? Let AI write the prompt

Say what you want in your own words. Read the prompt Claude writes, fix anything wrong, then paste it back. Works for any step below.

Write-my-prompt prompt · Claude
Write me a clear prompt I can paste back here to change my deck.
What I want: [say it in your own words, for example: a cleaner blue theme and bigger numbers on slide 2]
Keep the prompt short.

Step 3: add a chart and an icon

Claude can't lift a chart out of a PDF, so it redraws one from the dashboard's numbers. You get a real chart you can edit in PowerPoint.

Chart and icon · Claude
On the slide about what sells, add a bar chart of net sales by brand, highest to lowest.
Use the exact numbers in my dashboard. Label each bar with its number and start the bars at zero.
On the title slide, add a simple phone icon.
Keep all other slides the same.
Give me a new version of the PowerPoint file.

Claude draws charts and simple icons. For a photo, use Google Slides: Insert → Image → Search the web.

One slide only? Name it, and say what stays: “Change only slide 3. Keep all other slides exactly as they are.”

7Check it before Jordan presents

Notes and charts can bring in new numbers. Check after you modify.

Check prompt · Claude
List every number on the slides and in the speaker notes.
Next to each one, say where in my dashboard it came from.
If it did not come from my dashboard, say so.

Five checks before you present

  1. Facts: every number matches the dashboard. (Apple: $56,293.)
  2. Gaps: no [NEED] left on a slide.
  3. Story: read only the titles. They tell the plan.
  4. Notes: you'd say every line out loud.
  5. Clarity: one message per slide. Readable from the back.

8Gemini Canvas → Google Slides

  1. Open gemini.google.com. In the prompt bar, open Tools and pick Canvas.
  2. Copy the prompt below. Replace the last line with your fixed outline from Claude. Send.
  3. Ask for one change in the chat, for example warmer colors.
  4. Click Share & export → Export to Slides. Your deck opens in Google Slides.
Canvas prompt · Gemini Canvas
Create a 6-slide presentation from this outline.
Keep my slide titles exactly. Add speaker notes to each slide.
Use a clean, simple style that fits the business.
Do not add any number that is not in my outline. Keep every [NEED] tag.

[paste your fixed outline here]

Finish in Google Slides

  1. Theme: Slide → Change theme. Every slide follows.
  2. Photo: Insert → Image → Search the web. Try “smartphone store” for the title slide.
  3. Notes: View → Show speaker notes. Missing? Paste them from Claude.
  4. Practice: Slideshow → Presenter view. You see your notes; the room sees the slides.
