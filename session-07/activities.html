Why decks

WatchDeck slides 3–4

A document is read alone and kept. A deck is shown in a room while someone talks, so something happens in that room: a yes, a plan, a new skill.

DocumentDeck
ReaderReads alone, at their own paceListens to you, glances at the screen

GoalA record to come back toSomething happens in the room

Five jobs a deck does

ASKGet a yes: proposal, budget request, pitch to a bank
TEACHBuild a skill: training, how-to steps, orientation
REPORTShow where things stand: monthly numbers, status update
PLANGet everyone on one plan: kickoff, goals, holiday plan
RALLYMove a crowd: town hall, fundraiser, welcome

What makes a good deck

  1. One message per slide, and the title says it. “Online discounts are five times deeper than in store”, not “Online discounts”.
  2. The slide is for the room. The notes are for you. A title and up to 3 short points on screen.
  3. It ends with the ask. What you want the room to decide or do.
  4. Fewer true slides beat more impressive ones. Cut any slide you can't stand behind.

WatchDeck slides 7–9

Today's deck

Maya, owner of Corner Café, presents the holiday plan to her two café leads (Austin and Nashville): what to restock and promote, and whether online discounts are too deep. 6 slides, ending with two 2-week tests.

The title test: read only the titles. Could someone repeat the plan? A topic title (“Sales”) says nothing. A message title (“Whole bean coffee is half our sales”) says the point.

The [NEED] rule: if something is missing, AI writes [NEED: what is missing] instead of guessing. It's already in every prompt today. Fill every [NEED] yourself, or cut the line.

Parts of a deck

Watch + quizDeck slides 14–16

Look at the deck you just built. If you can name the part, you can ask AI to change it. Which part is still empty?

  • Theme colors, fonts and background for the whole deck
  • Layouts where things sit: title only, two columns, big picture
  • Slides the pages, in order, one message each
  • Elements title, text, chart, picture, icon, slide number
  • Speaker notes what you'll say. Only you see them

Slide types: Title · Agenda · Section · Big number · Chart · Comparison · Picture · Closing ask. Name the type in your prompt: “Make slide 3 a comparison slide.”
